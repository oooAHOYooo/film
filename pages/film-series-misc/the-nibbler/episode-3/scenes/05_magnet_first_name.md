EXT. BOATYARD - CONTINUOUS

Gael reaches into his jacket and takes out a small DEVICE — matte, no lights, the size of a cigarette case. He handles it like a light switch.

He presses it.

A deep electromagnetic THUMP. Every gun in the team's possession WRENCHES free — Dustin's pistol, the Ghost's sidearm, the Mayor's borrowed revolver — and streaks through the air to SLAM against a rusted crane column behind Gael. Lucy's earrings tug toward it hopefully. The guns hang on the steel, pinned, quivering.

LUCY

Our guns! He's—

(recalibrating)

—okay. Yes. Clearly.

MAYOR ANDERSON

We may have lost our weapons. But we still have each other.

LUCY

(staring at him)

Who talks like that?

Gael pockets the device. It was not a dramatic gesture. It was housekeeping.

GAEL

(to the Mayor, courteous)

Mister Mayor. He watches your press conferences. He wants you to know that.

The Mayor goes pale. Gael turns, finally, fully, to Dustin. The yard is silent. Twelve weevil masks watching, blank as dinner plates.

GAEL

You should have stayed out of this, Dustin.

Not Detective. Not Meglio. Not Nibbler.

Dustin. First name. Steady. Unhurried. Like he has been waiting a long time to say it out loud, and saying it no longer feels like anything at all.

The Ghost's head turns — a slow, precise degree — from Gael to Dustin.

Dustin doesn't look back at him. Doesn't look at anyone but Gael.

DUSTIN

(quietly)

You know I couldn't.

GAEL

(a breath; almost a smile)

Yes. I know.

Gael lifts two fingers. The ring of weevils raises its bats in one motion.

FADE OUT.
