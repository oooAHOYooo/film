# SOLO

**Working Title:** Solo  
**Starring:** Samuel Chen  
**Runtime Target:** 90 minutes  
**Shoot Window:** Winter 2027  
**Format:** One-file film draft

---

## Core Idea

A quiet, character-driven feature about a young man who tries to keep his life small, private, and controllable until one winter forces him to choose between isolation and connection.

Samuel Chen plays the lead, a precise and observant man whose habits are built around staying alone. He can handle work, errands, routines, and small talk, but not the parts of life that ask him to be fully seen. The film follows him through one cold season as the world around him keeps nudging him toward other people.

## Logline

In the winter of 2027, a guarded man who has built his life around being left alone is pulled into an unexpected relationship that threatens the routine he uses to survive.

## Tone

- Intimate
- Observational
- Wintery and restrained
- Human, not sentimental
- Small moments that build into a larger emotional turn

## Story Shape

The film is built as a single continuous treatment rather than a scene system.

### Beginning

We meet the lead in his regular life. He is competent, careful, and somewhat invisible on purpose. He lives with structure because structure keeps him from drifting into feelings he does not want to deal with. Winter makes that isolation feel sharper. The days are short, the streets are bare, and every errand feels a little too exposed.

Something interrupts his routine. It may be a person, an obligation, a family complication, or a practical problem he cannot ignore. The important thing is that he cannot solve it by withdrawing.

### Middle

The lead is forced into repeated contact with another person or small group. At first he treats the connection as temporary. He keeps boundaries, short answers, and exits ready.

But the longer the film goes, the more the relationship reveals what his isolation has cost him. He starts to notice how much of his life has been built around not needing anyone. Winter becomes a pressure system around that choice. He is not just lonely; he is practiced at avoiding the conditions that would let him be known.

This section should hold the film’s emotional weight. The external plot stays modest, while the internal shift grows.

### End

By the end, he has to decide whether to return to the life he built or let something new in. The resolution should feel earned, quiet, and human. Not a grand transformation, but a real one.

## Character

### Lead

The lead is an adult man with a controlled presence. He does not talk much unless he has to. He notices details. He prefers systems over surprises. He is not cold exactly, but he has made being alone into a skill.

Samuel Chen should play him with restraint and precision, letting small reactions carry the emotional meaning.

### Supporting Cast

- A person who keeps crossing his path and refuses to let him stay sealed off
- A family member, coworker, neighbor, or old friend who understands him better than he wants
- A secondary figure who reflects the life he might have had if he made different choices

## Setting

Winter 2027. The story should feel grounded in a lived-in place with cold weather, quiet streets, muted interiors, and enough texture to make being alone feel physical.

The environment matters as much as the dialogue:

- Empty parking lots
- Dim kitchens
- Frosted windows
- Long drives
- Early sunsets
- Rooms that feel too still

## Themes

- Isolation vs. connection
- Habit as a survival strategy
- The cost of emotional control
- Choosing to be seen
- What winter reveals when everything else gets stripped away

## Production Notes

- Keep it economical
- Let performance do most of the work
- Avoid over-plotting
- Give the film room for silence
- Winter should be visible in every frame
