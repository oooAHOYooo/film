## Cinematography Specifications — Wide Shot Emphasis

### **Wide Shot Strategy by Location**

#### **Boatyard (E1_09, E3_04-08)**
- **Drone/Aerial Wide:** Entire boatyard perimeter establishing (4K for future editorial)
- **Ground-Level Wides:** 50-100mm long-lens compression, Dustin small in dock landscape
- **Water Reflections:** Polarizer, tungsten, late-night industrial beauty
- **Arena Wide:** Fight sequence maintains constant wide medium-shots (never go tighter than medium)
- **Silent Wides:** Empty boatyard post-action, water lapping, rope creaking (sound design space)

#### **Grand Ballroom (E2_01-05)**
- **Overhead Chandelier:** High-mounted gimbal wide shots downward into crowd
- **Stage-to-Audience:** 35-50mm at stage level, crowd depth (450+ people implied)
- **Crowd Parting:** Dolly/steadicam through crowd maintaining ballroom frame edges
- **Screen Reflections:** Wide shots capturing both screens AND reflected crowd faces
- **Sight-Line Wides:** Ballroom width allows sight-line composition (Dustin sees Gael across room, both in frame)

#### **Police Office (E1_01, E1_07, E2_06, E3_01-03)**
- **Overhead Desk:** Mounted camera looking down at case files, evidence, Dustin's hands
- **Office Deep-Focus:** Wide angle allowing entire office in sharp focus (case files, doubters, mayor)
- **Fluorescent Naturalism:** Avoid mood lighting; fluorescent is the mood
- **Wide Framing:** Four-person scenes in single wide (not coverage-based shooting)

#### **Street Exteriors (E1_02, E1_06, E3_09)**
- **Trail of Glasses Wide:** Long-lens compression down city block, Dustin following (dot in landscape)
- **Alley Wide:** Actor dwarfed by alley geometry, shadow work, practical light
- **Dustin Alone:** Final epilogue — wide street, Dustin walking away, city recedes around him
- **Establishing Wides:** Multiple passes of key streets for opening/closing sequences

#### **Shipyard Flashback (E1_04)**
- **Desaturated Wide:** Entire yard in frame, broken-glass field landscape
- **Standoff Composition:** Dustin and Weevil across yard distance (power dynamic through scale)
- **Cold Color Grade:** Blue-cyan cast, hard practical light, minimal fill

## Schedule Contingencies

### **If Weather Delays Exterior Work (E1_02, E1_06, E3_09)**
- **Backup Plan:** Move to interior ballroom/office days (can be shot regardless of weather)
- **Day Swap:** Push E1_02 Trail of Glasses to Day 11 interior reserve time if rain

### **If Crowd Extras Unavailable for Ballroom (E2_03-05)**
- **Contingency:** Shoot gala with smaller crowd (100-150), use extra passes of same extras, shoot tighter medium-shots
- **VFX Add:** Crowd build-out in post (modern standard for period noir)

### **If Boatyard Equipment Fails (E3_04-08)**
- **Alternative:** Push boatyard action to wrap-up days, use double-day (14-16 hour) schedule if needed
- **Critical Path:** Boatyard is location-dependent; cannot be rescheduled to interior

## Production Design Notes — Wide-Shot Readability

- **Detective Office:** Every object must read at 10+ feet back (case folders large/visible, evidence board high-contrast)
- **Ballroom:** Banners/signage must read in wide-shots; crowd extras given clear blocking (not random)
- **Boatyard:** Every shipping container labeled; dock numbers visible; industrial geometry emphasized for scale
- **Rudie's Bar:** Neon signs bright enough to read in wide-shots; practical bottles/glassware high-contrast against dark wood

## Final Production Notes

**Shooting Philosophy:** This is a **noir that breathes**. Wide shots are not compositional luxury — they're narrative. Every wide establishes Dustin as isolated figure in a city that contains him. The boatyard and ballroom are characters themselves. Gael moves through space with Colombian unhurried grace (no quick cuts, no panic). Voiceover carries the weight that visuals cannot. 

Every day prioritizes **establishing coverage** over insert close-ups. Trust that the wide frame contains the performance. Cut conservatively in post.
