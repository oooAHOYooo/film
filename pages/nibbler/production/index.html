Micro-Budget Approach

All actors are unpaid friends committed to the project. All locations are volunteer owners providing free access. Budget covers fuel, minimal catering, permits, and consumables only. Equipment is borrowed from friends. Post-production (editing, color, sound) is director-led using free/affordable software. This is a pure passion project and proof of concept for what's possible with commitment and practical cinematography.

Cast

Dustin Meglio (The Nibbler): Detective protagonist, voiceover narrator. Carries entire series on noir monologue. Must deliver strong voiceover performance (practice before shoot). Available all 14 days.

Gael: Colombian operative, field commander for the Weevil. Precise, unhurried, dangerous. First-name moment with Dustin is emotional climax of Episode 3. Fight choreography capability required. Days 2, 4, 7-11.

Max "The Ghost": Dustin's trusted partner. Minimalist presence-based performance (speaks rarely). Loyalty through observation. Most shoot days except boatyard-light periods.

Lucy: Bartender/insider, sharp wit. Reads people faster than anyone. Team member by Episode 2. Fight choreography for boatyard rescue. Days 3-4, 7-11, 8-9.

Mayor Anderson: Mayor, Gael's brother, revelation catalyst. Carries heavy emotional scene (office confession). Days 4, 7-11, 8-9.

Production Approach

Strengths: Noir thrives on practical locations and available light (boatyard darkness, office fluorescents, bar neon, streetlights). Strong dialogue and voiceover carry DIY aesthetic. All-volunteer cast = genuine commitment. Simple locations = authentic authenticity. Script quality + editing > visual polish.

Constraints: No reshoot budget (get it right on set). Friend availability matters (schedule across 14 days). Night shoots are exhausting (Days 2, 8-9). No massive crowd (20-30 friends max, not 500 extras). No professional color grading (in-camera white balance + minimal post). Audio is everything (clean dialogue, strong voiceover).

Key Success Factors: (1) Lock cast + locations NOW with written confirmation. (2) Detailed shot list (max 15-20 shots/day). (3) Boatyard fight choreography rehearsal (2-3 times before shoot). (4) Good lav mic setup (production audio is critical). (5) Tight editorial discipline in post-production. (6) Trust the voiceover to carry limitations.

Critical Recommendations

🎬

Lock Everything Early

Contact cast + location owners immediately. Get written confirmation. No last-minute surprises. Boatyard access, bar hours, office availability must be confirmed now.

🧪

Test Equipment First

Borrow camera/audio/lights NOW. Test everything before shoot. Ensure SD cards work, batteries charge, microphone captures clean audio. No day-of surprises.

📋

Detailed Shot List

Director creates max 15-20 shots per day. Tight schedule requires no waste. Know exactly what you need before you shoot. Keeps energy high, prevents overthinking.

🥊

Rehearse Fight Choreography

Boatyard action (Days 8-9) requires 2-3 practice runs with henchmen friends. Know the dance before cameras roll. Safety first, then efficiency.

🎙️

Protect Audio Quality

Production audio is everything on low-budget. Good lav mic setup = less post-work. Clean dialogue takes = strong final mix. Audio carries what visuals cannot.

⚡

Manage Energy

14 days is exhausting. Build lighter days between intense ones. Night shoots are hardest (Days 2, 8-9). Feed your crew. Keep morale high. Friends take care of friends.
