# The Nibbler — Episode Treatment
**"Shadows of Deception" · Three-Episode Mini Series**
Written for the screen by Alex Gonzalez

---

## Series Logline

In New Haven's shadow economy of secrets and favors, detective Dustin Meglio — known on the streets as The Nibbler — follows a trail of small, baffling clues straight into the biggest conspiracy the city has seen: a mayoral scandal, a phantom criminal who leaves weevil figurines at every crime scene, and a revelation that will shake Dustin to his foundation — because the man behind the mask is someone he already knows.

---

## Noir Foundation

This is a film noir. New Haven is the city — familiar, decaying at the edges, still beautiful if you know where to look. The story runs on voiceover, shadow, moral ambiguity, and the specific texture of a man who chose justice and now wonders what that costs him. Dustin Meglio is not invulnerable. Gael is not simply evil. The Mayor is not simply corrupt. Everyone in this story has a secret and a reason.

**Tone:** Dark, dry, occasionally funny. Dustin's voiceover carries wit under the weight. The Ghost is laconic. Lucy is sardonic. The Mayor is exhausted. Gael believes he is righteous — and he may be, at least in part.

**The personal thread:** What elevates this above procedural noir is the connection between Dustin and Gael. They share a past. The exact shape of that past is the mystery underneath the mystery — the audience will feel it before they can name it. Gael's crimes are not random. His targets are chosen. His calling cards — the weevil figurines — are messages, and some of them are meant specifically for Dustin.

---

## Character Hierarchy

### The Weevil (Mark Anderson)

The mastermind. The Mayor's estranged brother — sent to Maine, escaped, took the family fortune, built a following around a genuine ideology of chaos and reconstruction. He operates from the shadows. In Series One he is mostly heard, rarely seen. The grand ballroom stunt is the closest he comes to stepping into the light. He is the architect. Gael is the blade.

**His ideology — "New New Haven":** Mark is a misguided utopian whose original grievance was legitimate. New Haven's real problem: over 60% of city property is tax-exempt, nearly half of that Yale's — one of the world's richest institutions in one of Connecticut's poorest cities. The PILOT payments Yale makes cover a fraction of what fair taxation would produce. The city can't fund its schools. Residents march on Yale over hunger and homelessness. Mark grew up watching this and watched his brother, the Mayor, learn to *navigate* it rather than fight it.

His rhetoric sounds like genuine progressive urban politics — housing equity, community land trusts, accountability for entrenched institutional power. He speaks the same language as New Haven's real reform tradition. The irony is that it's borrowed from the playbook of people who tried the legitimate route and got incremental results at best.

**The Adams parallel — and the Mayor's Japan scandal:** Mark's most powerful weapon against his brother is the Japan scandal: a foreign entanglement, money and favors crossing borders, a man who claimed to serve New Haven while doing business abroad. This is the exact shape of the fall of NYC Mayor Eric Adams — indicted in 2024 for taking bribes from Turkish government officials (luxury travel, hotel stays, favors for foreign nationals) while branding himself the working-class champion. The charges were eventually dismissed. The pattern wasn't.

Mayor Anderson's Japan scandal is that pattern in miniature. Mark knows it. He weaponizes it at the Grand Ballroom.

**The deepest irony:** Mark is running the same system he's attacking. Cash-only network. Personal loyalty enforced by reward and dependency. An operation that answers only to him. He has mistaken *power over the system* for *change of the system.* The people in his network who believe in him aren't wrong to want what he's promised — they're wrong to believe he's the one who'll deliver it. He is building a fiefdom with better rhetoric.

---

### Gael — The Weevil's Operative

**Not the Weevil. Works for the Weevil.**

Gael is the human face of the Weevil's operation — the field commander, the fixer, the one who executes. While the Weevil plans from the shadows, Gael moves through New Haven with total ease: placing the figurines, running the henchmen, managing the network. He is the one Dustin keeps almost catching. He is the one who calls Dustin by his first name.

**Colombian.** Gael is from Colombia — carries it with him not as a costume but as a way of being. Precise. Unhurried. Stylish without being flashy. He brings a South American elegance to the operation that the Weevil's other associates lack. He speaks when he needs to and goes quiet in a way that fills the room. There is a warmth underneath the danger — the kind that makes people trust him before they realize they shouldn't. New Haven's Colombian and broader Latino community gravitates toward him, not because he exploits that connection, but because he actually understands it.

**His relationship to the Weevil's ideology:** Gael is not a true believer. He's a professional. He may respect the Weevil's intelligence. He may even find the mission useful. But he's playing a longer game and it doesn't entirely overlap with the Weevil's. This ambiguity is intentional — Series Two will complicate the question of where Gael's actual loyalty lies.

**The past with Dustin:** Something happened between them long before Gael was working for anyone. The series seeds this quietly — Dustin's voiceover suppresses something when he first describes the figurines, as if he recognizes the craftwork. At the ballroom, Gael's operation includes a detail that only someone who knew Dustin's history would know to include. At the boatyard, Gael calls him *Dustin* — not Detective. First name. Unhurried. Like he's been waiting to say it for a while.

What exactly happened between them is the engine for Series Two. It is not resolved in Series One. It is only confirmed that it exists.

**Signature:** The weevil figurines are placed by Gael, not the Weevil himself. Small, intricately designed, cold to the touch. Some are left for the authorities. Some are left where only Dustin will look.

## Episode 1: "The Trail"

**~22 minutes · Act 1 of the series**

### Cold Open

A crime scene. Dustin Meglio's eyes move behind a magnifying glass — slow, deliberate. He dusts for prints with tweezers, drops a sample in his coat pocket. Dramatic score underneath. His voiceover begins:

> *The city has changed. It used to have character. Now it only has mischief. I used to walk in the bright streets. Now I lurk in the shadows — waiting for a chance to catch a break.*

We are in the dim corridor of a private office, flickering fluorescent light. Dustin is at his desk, case files spread around him. He tells us about The Weevil — an elusive figure behind a series of high-profile robberies, operating outside the law, leaving behind a trail of chaos and a calling card: a small, intricately designed metal weevil figurine.

He pauses on one figurine in the evidence bag. His voiceover drops slightly.

> *The figurines bothered me more than they should have. Not because of what they were. Because of the way they were placed. Like they were left for someone specific.*

He doesn't finish the thought.

### The Trail of Glasses

Dustin steps outside the station for a chewing tobacco break. He doesn't like the taste and spits it out almost immediately. As he does, he spots something: a pair of glasses on the sidewalk. Then another. Then another — a trail of them stretching down the block.

He follows.

> *One thing was certain — whoever these belonged to had a serious problem with their vision. And in a way, so did I.*

The trail ends at a local pub.

### Rudie's Bar

Dustin settles in. Whiskey. The regulars give him the look — the one that says *there goes the Nibbler*. Women smile. Men nod. He's a bit of a neighborhood institution and he knows it, even if it embarrasses him a little.

He picks up a tattered newspaper off the bar. Headline: **"Mayor's Japan Scandal Resurfaces: A Dark Secret Threatens New Haven's Future."**

His eyes narrow. He reads. His voiceover, almost to himself:

> *The mayor's scandal with Japan. More than a stain on his reputation. The Weevil is in this — I can feel it. He's pulling strings. The scandal is just another pawn in his game.*

A beat. Then:

> *The Weevil. I keep calling him that. Like he's a stranger.*

He folds the paper. Doesn't finish the thought.

### Shipyard Flashback

Cold filter. The abandoned shipyard. Dustin had him. The Weevil, sitting behind a makeshift desk in the middle of the yard, surrounded by shards of broken glasses. Dustin drew his weapon. The Weevil looked up.

For just a half-second — a flicker of recognition between them. Then: puff of smoke. Gone.

Dustin is left holding handcuffs and a pistol in an empty yard.

Back at the bar, present tense. Dustin tells the bartender, Lance, what happened. Lance says it's okay. Dustin asks: *Is it?*

### The Ghost Walks In

Max, known as The Ghost, enters without announcement. The bar shifts — people notice. He joins Dustin. They drink.

> *Max. My trusted partner. Elusive, mysterious. His loyalty is unwavering. I've never been entirely sure why he chose this life after everything it cost him.*

Ghost asks about the Weevil. Dustin describes him — the precision, the theatricality, the disappearing act. He describes the eyes. He almost says something more. He doesn't.

Later, behind Rudie's, in the alley: Dustin tells the whole story. Ghost leans in. They agree: they're going after him. Ghost produces high-tech surveillance gear from his coat.

### Detective Office — The Doubters

Morning. Dustin presents what he has. Detectives Jones and Miller are already laughing.

> **JONES:** Come on, Dustin. Disappearing into thin air? You've been chasing shadows too long.

The boss shuts Miller up. He tells Dustin he's the town's hero, fine, but what does he actually have? Dustin says they know where the Weevil is moving. The boss looks at him for a long moment.

*Go find him. Don't embarrass us.*

### Outside the Station

Dustin chews more tobacco. Spits it out again.

> **DUSTIN:** Maybe they're right. The chances of us catching the Weevil—
> **GHOST:** Are you kidding me? You're the Nibbler. You're the reason I got into this.

Dustin looks at the gear. Nods.

### Boatyard Surveillance — Night

They set up in a dark corner of the boatyard. Equipment hum, water lapping. A signal breaks through the noise. Ghost unscrambles it.

Two words on screen:

**MAYOR GRAND BALLROOM**

Dustin and Ghost exchange a look.

> **DUSTIN:** I've got an idea.

*FADE OUT. End of Episode 1.*

---

**What Episode 1 plants:**
- Dustin's character, world, and reputation
- The Weevil as a genuine phantom — dangerous and theatrical
- Two moments of suppressed recognition: the figurines, the eyes at the shipyard. The audience doesn't know what they mean yet. Dustin does.
- The Japan scandal as the thread connecting everything
- The boatyard signal as the series' first real lead

## Episode 2: "The Party"

**~22 minutes · Act 2 of the series**

### Lucy

Dustin and Ghost need inside the Mayor's event — a public gala at City Hall. They need someone who knows the service side: staff rotations, catering contacts, back entrances.

They find Lucy at a seaside pub. A noisy night — sailors singing shanties, glasses clinking. Lucy is working the bar, completely unbothered by the chaos around her.

Dustin calls out to her over the noise. Ghost introduces himself. They find a booth.

Dustin explains what they need. Lucy listens, expressionless.

> **LUCY:** Alright, enough of that. What do I have to do?

Dustin hesitates — he doesn't want to put her in danger. Lucy puts a hand on his arm.

> **LUCY:** Dustin. I've seen the shady dealings behind closed doors. This is my chance to make a difference. Trust me, I can handle it.

Ghost nods. The three of them clasp hands.

### The Grand Ballroom

City Hall. Banners. Suits. Champagne. Mayor Anderson takes the stage to applause.

Gael is already in the room. Disguised, blending perfectly, he slips through the crowd to backstage. He finds a technician. Whispers something.

The screens on either side of the stage flicker to life. Private footage — the Mayor in backroom meetings, cash handoffs, whispered arrangements. The crowd goes silent. The Mayor stammers.

Then Gael steps onto the stage. Composed. Almost serene.

> **GAEL (as The Weevil):** Ladies and gentlemen. It seems your Mayor's true nature has been exposed.

The crowd erupts. And as he turns to survey the chaos he's engineered — for just a moment, from his spot in the room, Dustin sees his face.

Dustin's voiceover, very quiet:

> *There he was. And I knew him. I had always known him.*

### The Detective Office — The Team Watches

Dustin, Ghost, and Lucy watch the ballroom chaos on screens.

> **LUCY:** The Weevil's true motive — it's not just crime. He wants to humiliate the Mayor. To bring him down in front of everyone.

Dustin is already standing. Something has shifted in him — this is no longer just a case.

### Backstage — The Mayor

They move fast. Backstage. Mayor Anderson is alone, pale, shaken.

Dustin approaches. The Mayor looks up. Lucy tells him: he was set up. The Weevil is targeting him. They're here to stop it.

The Mayor looks at Dustin.

> **MAYOR ANDERSON:** I've always trusted your team. And now I need you more than ever.

He pauses.

> **MAYOR ANDERSON:** Rob. There's one more thing. Come meet me in my office.

*FADE OUT. End of Episode 2.*

---

**What Episode 2 deepens:**
- Lucy as a full team member — essential, not decorative
- Gael revealed as a strategist: this was always about the Mayor, never just robbery
- Dustin's moment of recognition at the ballroom — the audience now knows he knows. The Mayor's private knowledge is Episode 3's engine.

## Episode 3: "Blood and Weevils"

**~22 minutes · Act 3 of the series**

### The Mayor's Office — The Truth

Quiet. Dark wood. Framed photographs. The Mayor sits across from Dustin.

> **MAYOR ANDERSON:** Dustin. I need to tell you the truth about The Weevil. He's my brother. We called him Mark. But that was never really his name.

Dustin absorbs this.

The Mayor talks about Gael like someone recounting a wound that never healed. Growing up together. The resentment. The Mayor rose. Gael deteriorated — mental health, instability, the wrong choices and a system that failed him. The family sent him to Maine. They told themselves it was for his good.

Gael escaped. Took the family fortune. Built something. A following. People with genuine grievances who believe in his vision: tear the city down and build it again from the truth.

> **MAYOR ANDERSON:** He was always the smarter one. I was just the one people liked.

His voice breaks.

> **MAYOR ANDERSON:** I failed him, Dustin. I failed as a brother.

Dustin is quiet for a long moment. Then:

> **DUSTIN:** Mayor. What's done is done. What's now — is now.

A beat. Then Dustin adds, almost to himself:

> **DUSTIN:** I knew him. A long time ago. Before all this.

He doesn't elaborate. The Mayor searches his face. Dustin is already standing.

### The Alliance

The four of them — Dustin, Ghost, Lucy, Mayor Anderson — in the office. The Mayor proposes it formally: a team. A public alliance dedicated to taking down Gael's network and whatever he's been building.

Dustin is in before he finishes the sentence.

### The Boatyard — The Trap

Night. The team moves through the boatyard. All four, watching the dark. Salt air. Creaking docks.

They are not alone.

The Weevil's henchmen step out from the shadows — weevil costumes, bats, the full pageant of it. A ring closes. Then the man running the operation steps forward from the back of the circle, calm and unhurried. Not in a weevil costume. A suit. Gael.

He looks at Dustin with something that isn't quite satisfaction and isn't quite regret. The magnet he triggers pulls the team's guns clean off them and sends them clattering to the ground.

> **GAEL:** You should have stayed out of this, Dustin.

Not *Detective*. Dustin. First name. Steady. Like they've had this conversation before in some other form.

The Ghost looks at Dustin. Dustin doesn't look back.

They fight. Hand to hand — the team for the first time working as a unit. The henchmen take damage. And Gael slips the circle with a Colombian's unhurried exit — no scrambling, no panic, just gone, the way someone disappears when they've planned for every outcome.

As his crew retreats they drag civilians — hostages — toward the dock exit.

Lucy sees it first:

> **LUCY:** We can't let them get away with innocent lives.

The team pivots. They chase, free the hostages. Henchmen scatter. But Gael is gone.

### Aftermath

The team stands in the quiet boatyard. Catching their breath. The Mayor looks at the dark water.

> **MAYOR ANDERSON:** We may not have captured him tonight. But we saved innocent lives. Our fight continues.

The Ghost looks at Dustin.

> **GHOST:** He called you by your first name.

Dustin doesn't answer.

### Final Scene — The Office, The Handshake

Mayor's office. Daylight. The team assembled. The Mayor extends his hand. Dustin takes it.

> **MAYOR ANDERSON:** Together, we'll be unstoppable.

Last shot: Dustin, alone, leaving. He steps onto the street. Voiceover — quieter now, carrying more weight than in Episode 1:

> *The city still has shadows. Gael is still out there. He called me by my name. That means he's been watching. That means whatever this is — it didn't start at the shipyard. It started long before that. I don't know what he wants from me yet. But I know this — the Nibbler has a crew now. And whatever Gael is building, we're going to find out before he finishes building it.*

*FADE TO BLACK.*

**END OF SERIES ONE.**

## Series Throughline — Noir Logic

| | Episode 1 | Episode 2 | Episode 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dustin's state** | Alone, mocked, suppressing something | In motion, on the edge of recognition | Named by Gael — the personal dimension breaks open |
| **The Weevil** | A name. A calling card. A rumor. | The architect of the ballroom stunt — still unseen | Confirmed as the Mayor's brother. Still in shadow. |
| **Gael** | The figurines. Glimpsed at the shipyard — something suppressed. | Runs the ballroom operation. Dustin sees his face. | Names Dustin at the boatyard. The relationship is confirmed. |
| **The Mayor** | A headline | A victim | A man with secrets who knew the Weevil — but did he know Gael? |
| **The crew** | Dustin + Ghost | + Lucy | + Mayor Anderson |
| **Does Gael get caught?** | No | No | No — and the way he leaves tells Dustin this is personal |

Gael is never caught in Series One. The Weevil is never fully unmasked. Both escape in different ways — one physically, one conceptually. That's the point. Series One is the setup.

## Series Two — What's Been Planted

By the end of Episode 3, the following questions are live and unanswered:

1. **How does Dustin know Gael?** The voiceover suppresses it in Episode 1. The boatyard moment confirms it. The exact history is deliberately unwritten — to be developed before Series Two goes into production.
2. **Why is Gael working for the Weevil?** He's Colombian, cultured, operating at a high level. What brought him to New Haven and into the Weevil's orbit? Is it money? Ideology? Something that connects back to Dustin?
3. **Is Gael actually loyal to the Weevil?** He executes flawlessly. But he also called Dustin by his first name in front of witnesses. That's either reckless or intentional. Gael is not reckless.
4. **What does the Japan scandal actually conceal?** The mayor was accused of stealing a prop. True? And does Gael know what really happened — and why would a Colombian operative in New Haven care?
5. **What is the Weevil building?** Three episodes establish the infrastructure: cash, followers, cult logic. Series Two maps it — and Gael is the key to understanding its scope.
6. **What is Dustin's suppressed past?** Something in his history connects to Gael. He hasn't told Ghost. He hasn't told anyone. But Ghost noticed the boatyard moment. He will ask eventually.

---

## Key Noir Elements to Protect in Production

- **Dustin's voiceover** is the spine. It carries the show. Every episode needs it to frame action and locate us emotionally. Without it the noir collapses into procedural.
- **Rudie's Bar** is the one fixed point. Dustin always finds his way back there.
- **Gael's theatricality** — smoke, magnets, costumes, grand gestures — is deliberate. He performs. Let it be a little absurd. That's the genre and that's the character.
- **The Ghost says very little.** His power is presence and loyalty. He is also the one who will eventually ask Dustin the question Dustin is avoiding.
- **Lucy is sharp, not just capable.** She reads people faster than anyone else in the room. In Series Two, she may be the one who figures out the Dustin-Gael connection before Dustin admits it.
- **The first-name moment at the boatyard** is the emotional climax of Series One. It should land quietly, not dramatically — the weight comes from what's been suppressed in episodes 1 and 2.
