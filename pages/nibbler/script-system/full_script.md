### Scene 1: Crime Scene & Voiceover

*EPISODE 1 — The Trail | ID: e1-crime-scene-voiceover | File: e1_01_crime_scene_voiceover.md*

---

<!-- summary: Dustin works a strange robbery scene, finds a weevil figurine, and introduces the Weevil case in voiceover. -->

INT. PAWN SHOP - WHALLEY AVENUE - NIGHT

Red and blue light sweeps through a broken storefront window. Uniformed officers stand around drinking coffee. Nothing about them is urgent.

At the back wall: DUSTIN MEGLIO, 40s, trench coat, a face that has practiced looking haunted in the mirror. He studies the wall through an enormous magnifying glass. Slow. Deliberate. Much closer to the wall than any human needs to be.

DUSTIN

(V.O.)

The city has changed. It used to have character. Now it only has mischief.

He snaps on a dusting glove. With tweezers, he lifts a single hair off the counter and drops it into his coat pocket. Not an evidence bag. His pocket.

DUSTIN

(V.O.)

I used to walk in the bright streets. Now I lurk in the shadows — waiting for a chance to catch a break.

A young OFFICER approaches, holding out an evidence bag.

OFFICER

Detective. We found another one.

Inside the bag: a small metal WEEVIL FIGURINE. Intricate. Polished. Beautiful, if you didn't know better.

Dustin takes the bag. Looks at it a long moment. Something crosses his face and he puts it away before it can be named.

DUSTIN

Register emptied. Safe untouched. Nothing broken but the window they came in through. And this.

OFFICER

What does it mean?

DUSTIN

It means somebody's saying hello.

INT. DUSTIN'S OFFICE - POLICE STATION - NIGHT

A dim private office. Flickering fluorescent light. Case files fanned across a cluttered desk. A corkboard with photographs: storefronts, warehouses, a marina. Six weevil figurines in six evidence bags, pinned in a neat row.

Dustin sits with the newest figurine, turning the bag in the light.

DUSTIN

(V.O.)

Eleven robberies in four months. High-profile. Clean. No prints, no witnesses, no pattern anyone downtown could see. Just the calling card — a little metal weevil, left behind at every scene. The papers gave him the name. The Weevil. It stuck.

He holds the bag closer. His voiceover drops, quieter.

DUSTIN

(V.O.)

The figurines bothered me more than they should have. Not because of what they were. Because of the way they were placed. Careful. At a height. Like they were left for someone specific.

He sets the bag down. Doesn't finish the thought.

FADE OUT.

### Scene 2: Trail of Glasses

*EPISODE 1 — The Trail | ID: e1-trail-of-glasses | File: e1_02_trail_of_glasses.md*

---

<!-- summary: Outside the station, Dustin spits out chewing tobacco, spots a trail of eyeglasses on the sidewalk, and follows it across New Haven to Rudie's Bar. -->

EXT. POLICE STATION - NEW HAVEN - DAY

Dustin stands on the station steps. He produces a pouch of chewing tobacco, packs a wad into his cheek with the gravity of a ritual.

He hates it immediately. He spits it out.

DUSTIN

(V.O.)

I'd been trying to pick up chewing tobacco for six months. Every detective needs a vice. Mine didn't agree with me.

As he spits, his eyes land on something on the sidewalk.

A PAIR OF EYEGLASSES. Folded. Sitting on the pavement like they were set down by a careful hand.

Dustin looks up the block. Ten yards on: ANOTHER PAIR.

Beyond that, catching the light: ANOTHER.

DUSTIN

(V.O.)

Life wasn't always this easy. There were years when nothing connected. One was two, two was three — none of it made sense. And then some mornings the city just hands you a trail.

EXT. NEW HAVEN STREETS - CONTINUOUS

Dustin follows the glasses down the block. He passes pedestrians who step around the glasses without seeing them. He kneels at a pair, picks it up, holds it to the light. Thick lenses. Scratched.

DUSTIN

(V.O.)

One thing was certain. Whoever these belonged to had a serious problem with their vision.

He pockets the pair and keeps walking.

DUSTIN

(V.O.)

And in a way — so did I.

The trail turns a corner. Dustin turns with it.

EXT. RUDIE'S BAR - DAY

The trail ends at the door of a low brick bar. A hand-painted sign: RUDIE'S. The last pair of glasses sits on the doormat, folded, facing out. Like it was waiting for him.

Dustin looks at the glasses. Looks at the door.

He goes in.

FADE OUT.

### Scene 3: Rudie's Bar

*EPISODE 1 — The Trail | ID: e1-rudies-bar | File: e1_03_rudies_bar.md*

---

<!-- summary: At Rudie's, the regulars greet the Nibbler; Dustin reads the headline about the Mayor's Japan scandal and feels the Weevil behind it. -->

INT. RUDIE'S BAR - DAY

Dim. Wood. The murmur of conversation and the clink of glasses. A neighborhood bar that has outlived every neighbor.

Dustin enters. The room registers him the way a room registers weather. A woman at the end of the bar smiles into her drink. Two men by the pool table nod without pausing their game.

DUSTIN

(V.O.)

Rudie's. The one fixed point in a changing city. They know me here. The women smile. The men nod. I'd be lying if I said I hated it.

Behind the bar: LANCE, 60s, a bartender built like a filing cabinet. He has a whiskey poured before Dustin reaches the stool.

LANCE

Detective.

DUSTIN

Lance.

Dustin sits. Drinks. He sets the folded pair of eyeglasses from the trail on the bar next to his glass, positions it, studies it.

Lance looks at the eyeglasses. Decides not to ask.

Dustin pulls a tattered NEWSPAPER across the bar. The headline, huge:

"MAYOR'S JAPAN SCANDAL RESURFACES: A DARK SECRET THREATENS NEW HAVEN'S FUTURE."

His eyes narrow. He reads.

DUSTIN

(V.O.)

The Mayor's scandal with Japan. Thirty years buried and now it's front page again. Somebody dug it up. Somebody wanted it on this bar, in this light, in front of these eyes.

He turns the page. A grainy photo of MAYOR ANDERSON at a ribbon-cutting, smiling the smile of a man who practices.

DUSTIN

(V.O.)

The Weevil is in this. I can feel it. He doesn't rob a city like ours for the money. He's pulling strings. The scandal is just another pawn in his game.

A beat. Dustin looks at his own reflection behind the bottles.

DUSTIN

(V.O.)

The Weevil. I keep calling him that. Like he's a stranger.

He folds the paper. Doesn't finish the thought.

LANCE

You want to talk about it?

DUSTIN

No.

LANCE

Okay.

Dustin drinks. The pool game clicks on behind him.

FADE OUT.

### Scene 4: Shipyard Flashback

*EPISODE 1 — The Trail | ID: e1-shipyard-flashback | File: e1_04_shipyard_flashback.md*

---

<!-- summary: Flashback — Dustin cornered a man he took for the Weevil at the abandoned shipyard; a flicker of recognition, a puff of smoke, and the man was gone. -->

INT. RUDIE'S BAR - CONTINUOUS

Dustin stares into his whiskey. The bar sounds fade under the score.

DUSTIN

(V.O.)

Three weeks ago I had him. That's the part nobody knows. I had him, and I stood there like a man reading a menu.

EXT. ABANDONED SHIPYARD - DUSK [FLASHBACK]

Cold filter. Wind. Rust. Gulls somewhere, unimpressed.

Dustin moves between hulls of dry-docked boats, following a scatter of BROKEN EYEGLASSES — frames, lenses, shards of glass catching the last light. The trail thickens.

It leads to the center of the yard, where — impossibly — a MAN sits behind a wooden desk. Just a desk, in the open air, papers weighted with a coffee cup. The man writes something. Small, black coffee. No hurry in him at all.

He is in a suit. Well cut. Clean shoes in a filthy yard.

Dustin draws his pistol with one hand and handcuffs with the other, a gesture he has clearly rehearsed.

DUSTIN

Freeze!

The man looks up.

A half-second. That's all it is. The man's eyes find Dustin's, and something passes between them — a flicker of recognition, going both directions. The man does not look afraid. He looks, if anything, glad.

DUSTIN

(quieter, almost a question)

...Freeze.

The man reaches — unhurried — for something under the desk.

A PUFF OF SMOKE erupts. Thick, theatrical, absurd.

When it clears: an empty desk. A cooling cup of coffee. Dustin alone in a shipyard, holding handcuffs and a pistol, arresting no one.

He stands there a long moment.

INT. RUDIE'S BAR - DAY [PRESENT]

Dustin, at the bar, still looking at his whiskey.

DUSTIN

I let him get away, Lance. He was in front of my eyes and I let him get away.

LANCE

That's okay, Dustin.

DUSTIN

Is it?

LANCE

You're the best there is.

DUSTIN

Oh, stop it. You're just saying that.

LANCE

Maybe so.

Lance moves off to another customer. Dustin turns the whiskey glass a quarter turn. His voiceover, low:

DUSTIN

(V.O.)

I told Lance I let him get away. What I didn't tell him was the half-second before the smoke. The way he looked at me.

A beat.

DUSTIN

(V.O.)

Like we'd met.

FADE OUT.

### Scene 5: The Ghost Walks In

*EPISODE 1 — The Trail | ID: e1-ghost-enters | File: e1_05_ghost_enters.md*

---

<!-- summary: Max, the Ghost, walks into Rudie's; Dustin describes the shipyard man's eyes and almost says more. -->

INT. RUDIE'S BAR - LATER

The door opens. Nobody announces anything, and yet the room shifts.

MAX — known on the street as THE GHOST — stands in the doorway. 40s. Long coat. He has the stillness of a man who has already checked the exits. The pool game pauses. A woman at the bar sits up a little straighter.

The Ghost crosses to Dustin and sits. He does not order. Lance brings him a beer anyway.

DUSTIN

(V.O.)

Max. The Ghost. My trusted partner — elusive, mysterious, loyal past the point of reason. He lost everything once for telling the truth. I've never been entirely sure why he chose this life after what it cost him. I've never asked. That's the arrangement.

They drink in silence for a moment. It is a comfortable silence, or at least a well-worn one.

GHOST

The pawn shop on Whalley.

DUSTIN

Eleven now.

GHOST

Figurine?

DUSTIN

Figurine.

The Ghost nods slowly. Drinks.

GHOST

Tell me about the shipyard.

Dustin's hand stops halfway to his glass.

DUSTIN

Who told you about the shipyard?

GHOST

You did. Just now.

Dustin exhales. Puts the glass down.

DUSTIN

He was sitting at a desk, Max. A desk. In the middle of the yard, like it was a Tuesday at the bank. Broken glasses all over the ground — his little trail, his little joke. I had the drop on him. Gun out, cuffs out.

GHOST

And?

DUSTIN

Smoke. A magician's exit. Gone.

GHOST

You saw his face.

A pause. Dustin looks at the bottles behind the bar.

DUSTIN

I saw his eyes. Calm. Not the calm of a man who doesn't care. The calm of a man who's already planned the next four moves.

Dustin opens his mouth to say something more — something specific — and closes it again.

GHOST

(watching him)

What.

DUSTIN

Nothing. Finish your beer.

FADE OUT.

### Scene 6: Back Alley Conversation

*EPISODE 1 — The Trail | ID: e1-back-alley | File: e1_06_back_alley.md*

---

<!-- summary: In the alley behind Rudie's, Dustin lays out the whole case; the Ghost produces surveillance gear and they agree to go after the Weevil. -->

EXT. BACK ALLEY - BEHIND RUDIE'S - NIGHT

A single bulb over the service door. Dumpster. Wet brick. Dustin and the Ghost stand in the little cone of light like two men who have chosen the least comfortable place in New Haven to talk.

DUSTIN

Eleven robberies. Every one of them clean. Register cash, small valuables, nothing traceable — but never the safe. You understand? He leaves the safe. The money isn't the point.

GHOST

Then what's the point?

DUSTIN

The figurines. The trail of glasses. The desk in the shipyard. He's not hiding, Max. He's performing. Every scene is staged for an audience.

GHOST

What audience?

Dustin doesn't answer that.

DUSTIN

And now the Mayor's Japan scandal crawls out of its grave and onto the front page — the same month the Weevil's jobs start circling City Hall contracts. That's not weather. That's somebody seeding clouds.

The Ghost considers this. He is quiet long enough that a normal person would fill the silence. Dustin, to his credit, waits.

GHOST

The department?

DUSTIN

The department thinks I've been chasing shadows too long. The department thinks a man can't disappear in a puff of smoke.

GHOST

A man can't.

DUSTIN

I was there.

GHOST

I know.

The Ghost reaches into his coat. From an interior lining that should not hold this much, he produces, one item at a time: a DIRECTIONAL MICROPHONE. A SIGNAL SCANNER. A COMPACT MONITOR wrapped in a dish towel.

He lays them on the closed dumpster lid like a surgeon's tray.

DUSTIN

Where do you get these things?

GHOST

I know a guy.

DUSTIN

You are the guy.

GHOST

I know.

Dustin picks up the scanner. Weighs it in his hand. Something in him settles — the self-pity draining out, the other thing coming back in.

DUSTIN

The robberies track the water. Warehouses, the marina, the boatyard twice. If he's moving product — or people — it's through the docks.

GHOST

Then we watch the docks.

DUSTIN

We watch the docks.

They look at each other. A silent vow passes between them, and both men would deny it was a vow.

FADE OUT.

### Scene 7: Detective Office — Doubters

*EPISODE 1 — The Trail | ID: e1-detective-office-doubters | File: e1_07_detective_office_doubters.md*

---

<!-- summary: Dustin presents the Weevil case to a room of doubters; Jones and Miller mock him; the Boss gives him a leash — go find him, don't embarrass us. -->

INT. DETECTIVE OFFICE - POLICE STATION - MORNING

Bullpen. Bad coffee. Dustin stands at a corkboard he has wheeled in from his office — photographs, newspaper clippings, six figurines in evidence bags, all of it connected by RED STRING. There is a great deal of red string.

Facing him: DETECTIVE JONES and DETECTIVE MILLER, feet up, already enjoying themselves. A few other detectives orbit with coffee. The Ghost leans against the far wall, arms crossed, watching everything and saying nothing.

DUSTIN

(pointing along the string)

The robberies. The city contracts. The Mayor's scandal resurfacing. There's a pattern here. Every job circles City Hall and touches the waterfront. He's building toward something.

DETECTIVE JONES

(laughing)

Come on, Dustin. Disappearing into thin air? You've been chasing shadows too long.

DETECTIVE MILLER

(smirking)

Maybe the little bug statues are leaving themselves. You check for tiny fingerprints?

Scattered laughter. Dustin's fists clench at his sides. He looks around the room for one ally and finds only the Ghost, who gives him nothing except the fact of still being there — which, from the Ghost, is a great deal.

The laughter dies because the DETECTIVE BOSS has entered. 60s. A man whose patience retired before he did.

DETECTIVE BOSS

Enough.

He studies the corkboard. Then Dustin.

DETECTIVE BOSS

Let's have some respect for the Nibbler, boys. Town's hero.

(beat)

But for what, lately? Eleven scenes and what do you actually have? A theory made of string.

DUSTIN

We know how he moves. The waterfront. We know where to watch.

The Boss looks at him for a long moment. It is the look of a man doing math he doesn't enjoy.

DETECTIVE BOSS

You know what your problem is, Meglio? You're the only man in this building who still thinks this job is a calling.

DUSTIN

Is that a no, sir?

DETECTIVE BOSS

(already walking away)

Go find him. Don't embarrass us.

Dustin gathers his string.

MILLER

(under his breath)

Happy hunting, Nibbler.

The Ghost peels off the wall and follows Dustin out.

FADE OUT.

### Scene 8: Outside the Station

*EPISODE 1 — The Trail | ID: e1-outside-station | File: e1_08_outside_station.md*

---

<!-- summary: Dustin doubts himself outside the station; the Ghost reminds him who he is. -->

EXT. POLICE STATION - DAY

Dustin stands on the steps with the corkboard under one arm. He packs a fresh wad of chewing tobacco into his cheek.

He holds it a full three seconds this time. Progress. Then he spits it out.

DUSTIN

Maybe they're right, Max. No prints. No face I can put a name to. The chances of us catching the Weevil—

GHOST

Are you kidding me?

Dustin looks over. The Ghost is not looking at him; he is looking out at the street, which somehow makes it worse.

GHOST

You're the Nibbler. You're the reason I got into this.

DUSTIN

You got into this because you had nothing left.

GHOST

I had nothing left, and then I read about a detective who followed a trail of pistachio shells for two miles and closed the Hartley case. Everyone has a reason. You're mine.

(beat)

Besides. We have all this gear. Might as well give it a spin.

Dustin looks down at the duffel of surveillance equipment at the Ghost's feet. Then out at the street. Somewhere out there, the water. The docks.

He nods.

DUSTIN

(V.O.)

That's the thing about a partner. On the days you don't believe your own legend, they believe it for you. It's an inefficient system. It works.

FADE OUT.

### Scene 9: Boatyard Surveillance

*EPISODE 1 — The Trail | ID: e1-boatyard-surveillance | File: e1_09_boatyard_surveillance.md*

---

<!-- summary: Night surveillance at the boatyard; a scrambled signal resolves into two words — MAYOR GRAND BALLROOM. -->

EXT. BOATYARD - NIGHT

Dark water. Masts creak. Halyards ting against aluminum in the wind. Dustin and the Ghost are wedged into a corner between two dry-docked hulls, surrounded by the Ghost's equipment: the scanner, the monitor, a thermos.

The rig hums. Nothing happens. It is the kind of stakeout where nothing has been happening for hours.

DUSTIN

(adjusting the camera a quarter-inch)

Alright, Ghost. Let's hope this leads us to the Weevil.

GHOST

Trust your instincts. You're the Nibbler.

DUSTIN

You can't just say that every time.

GHOST

It works every time.

Dustin pours coffee from the thermos. On the monitor: gray boats, gray water, gray night.

Then — the scanner CHIRPS.

A waveform jumps across the screen. Pulses. Rhythmic. Deliberate. Not harbor traffic.

DUSTIN

(sitting up)

That's it. That's a message, Ghost. Somebody's transmitting from inside the yard.

GHOST

Scrambled.

DUSTIN

Can you unscramble it?

The Ghost produces a small device from his coat and connects it to the rig with the serenity of a man plugging in a toaster. Taps the screen. Taps it again.

The waveform shudders — resolves — and the monitor goes black.

Then, in clean white letters, two words:

MAYOR GRAND BALLROOM

Neither man moves. The masts creak. The water laps.

GHOST

The gala. City Hall. Friday.

DUSTIN

Every suit in New Haven under one roof, and the Weevil just circled the date.

They look at each other in the glow of the monitor.

DUSTIN

I've got an idea.

GHOST

I was afraid of that.

FADE OUT.

### Scene 10: Find Lucy

*EPISODE 2 — The Party | ID: e2-find-lucy | File: e2_01_find_lucy.md*

---

<!-- summary: Dustin and the Ghost need a way inside the Mayor's gala; they head for the seaside pub to find Lucy. -->

INT. DUSTIN'S OFFICE - POLICE STATION - DAY

The corkboard again. A new item pinned dead center: an engraved INVITATION — "THE CITY OF NEW HAVEN CORDIALLY INVITES YOU — MAYORAL GALA — GRAND BALLROOM, CITY HALL." It is a photograph of an invitation. They do not have one.

DUSTIN

(V.O.)

The gala. Friday night. Four hundred guests, a guest list with a waiting list, and security that actually checks. The Weevil had a way in. We didn't.

The Ghost studies the photo.

GHOST

I can get us blueprints.

DUSTIN

Blueprints don't pour champagne. We need the service side. Staff rotations, catering contacts, which back doors get propped open when the kitchen overheats. We need somebody who's worked a hundred of these and knows where a room keeps its secrets.

GHOST

You know somebody.

DUSTIN

I know somebody.

The Ghost waits.

DUSTIN

She's not going to be excited to see me.

GHOST

They rarely are.

EXT. NEW HAVEN WATERFRONT - DUSK

Dustin and the Ghost walk the harbor road, coats snapping in the wind. Ahead, at the end of a pier: a low pub with steamed windows and light spilling out. Even from here, the sound of singing.

DUSTIN

(V.O.)

Lucy. Sharpest pair of eyes in the New Haven service industry. She's worked every ballroom, back kitchen, and cocktail hour in this city. People say things around waitresses they'd never say around cops. Lucy remembers all of it.

GHOST

What's the singing?

DUSTIN

Shanties. It's shanty night.

GHOST

(beat)

There's a shanty night?

DUSTIN

Every Thursday. Try to blend in.

FADE OUT.

### Scene 11: Seaside Pub

*EPISODE 2 — The Party | ID: e2-seaside-pub | File: e2_02_seaside_pub.md*

---

<!-- summary: At the seaside pub, over sea shanties, Dustin and the Ghost recruit Lucy; she cuts through the speech and joins the team. -->

INT. SEASIDE PUB - NIGHT

Chaos, but warm chaos. A crew of SAILORS pounds the tables through a sea shanty. Glasses slosh. Somebody has a concertina and, worse, knows how to use it.

Behind the bar, working the taps through all of it, completely unbothered: LUCY, 30s. She pours four beers with one hand and cuts off a drunk with the other, without breaking rhythm.

Dustin and the Ghost push through the crowd. The Ghost looks around the room like it might need to be defused.

DUSTIN

(shouting over the shanty)

Lucy!

Lucy clocks him. Her expression does not change one degree.

LUCY

(to the other bartender)

Cover me. The law's here and it wants something.

INT. SEASIDE PUB - CORNER BOOTH - CONTINUOUS

A booth away from the noise, but not away enough. The shanty rolls on behind them. Lucy sits across from Dustin and the Ghost, arms folded.

LUCY

You've got until the end of this song.

DUSTIN

The Mayor's gala. Friday. We need in — quiet, through the service side. Someone hit the boatyard signal traffic and City Hall is the target. We think something's going to happen in that ballroom, and we think nobody in that ballroom knows it.

LUCY

(to the Ghost)

Who's this?

GHOST

The Ghost.

LUCY

Of course you are.

DUSTIN

Lucy — you've worked the Grand Ballroom. You know the catering captains. You know the rotations. You could put us inside without a single suit noticing.

LUCY

(flat)

You want me to smuggle two grown detectives into the biggest event of the year, past City Hall security, during whatever it is you think is going to happen.

DUSTIN

Yes.

Lucy looks at them both. The shanty behind them hits its final chorus.

LUCY

Alright. Enough of that. What do I have to do?

Dustin blinks — he had a speech prepared. He hesitates.

DUSTIN

Lucy, I have to be straight with you. This could get dangerous. I can't ask you to—

LUCY

(hand on his arm)

Dustin. I've worked ten years of these parties. I've seen the shady dealings behind every closed door in this city — I just never had anybody worth telling. This is my chance to make a difference. Trust me. I can handle it.

A beat. The Ghost nods once — which, from the Ghost, is a standing ovation.

GHOST

Friday, then.

LUCY

Friday. You two are dishwashers.

DUSTIN

Dishwashers?

LUCY

You wanted invisible. Nobody in the history of City Hall has ever looked at a dishwasher.

The three of them clasp hands over the table — a small, absurd, entirely sincere ceremony. Behind them the sailors hit the last note and the pub roars.

FADE OUT.

### Scene 12: City Hall Gala

*EPISODE 2 — The Party | ID: e2-city-hall-gala | File: e2_03_city_hall_gala.md*

---

<!-- summary: The night of the gala — Lucy plants Dustin inside, the Ghost watches the feeds, Mayor Anderson takes the stage, and Gael moves through the room toward backstage. -->

INT. CITY HALL - GRAND BALLROOM - NIGHT

Chandeliers. Banners with the Mayor's name. Four hundred of New Haven's finest suits drinking champagne under a ceiling that cost more than a school.

MAYOR ANDERSON, 60s, silver-haired, charismatic in the way of a man who has been forgiven often, works the room toward the stage.

At the service door, in a white catering jacket, carrying a tray of empty glasses with visible discomfort: DUSTIN. Lucy passes him, balancing three trays effortlessly.

LUCY

(under her breath)

Stop scanning the room like a detective. Scan it like a dishwasher.

DUSTIN

How does a dishwasher scan a room?

LUCY

He doesn't. That's the point.

She flows on into the crowd.

INT. DETECTIVE OFFICE - NIGHT

Dark bullpen. The Ghost sits alone before a bank of monitors — the ballroom feeds, the corridors, the loading dock. A radio at his lips.

GHOST

(into radio)

Two feeds on the ballroom, one on the dock. You're covered.

INT. CITY HALL - GRAND BALLROOM - CONTINUOUS

The lights dim. A spotlight finds the podium. Applause as MAYOR ANDERSON takes the stage.

MAYOR ANDERSON

Ladies and gentlemen — thank you. We gather tonight to celebrate the progress this city has made...

The speech rolls on, polished and empty. The crowd applauds on schedule.

And through the crowd — against its grain, smooth as water finding a drain — moves A MAN IN A SUIT. Not flashy. Quality that doesn't announce itself. Clean shoes. He carries a champagne flute he never drinks from.

GAEL. Though no one in this room knows the name.

He passes a KNOT OF GUESTS and they part for him without noticing they did it. He nods at a security guard; the guard nods back for no reason the guard could explain.

INT. BACKSTAGE - CITY HALL - CONTINUOUS

Cables, road cases, a lighting board. A TECHNICIAN in headphones watches the stage feed.

Gael appears beside him — simply there, like he'd always been.

GAEL

(quietly)

The screens. When I signal.

He places something in the technician's hand. We don't see what. The technician looks at it, looks at Gael, and nods obediently.

Gael turns to the stage feed. On the little monitor, the Mayor beams at his city.

Gael checks his watch. Unhurried.

FADE OUT.

### Scene 13: The Screens Go Live

*EPISODE 2 — The Party | ID: e2-screens-go-live | File: e2_04_screens_go_live.md*

---

<!-- summary: The ballroom screens go live with secret footage of the Mayor; Gael steps out, composed, and delivers the Weevil's message. -->

INT. CITY HALL - GRAND BALLROOM - NIGHT

Mayor Anderson, mid-sentence at the podium—

MAYOR ANDERSON

—because this city's best days are not behind it, they are—

The sound system CRACKLES. The two giant SCREENS flanking the stage flicker.

And go live.

GRAINY FOOTAGE fills them: Mayor Anderson in a private room, accepting an envelope. Another angle, another night: a handshake with a man the papers would recognize. A hotel bar. Cash on a table. Timestamps in the corner like little verdicts.

The ballroom goes silent — that specific silence of four hundred people simultaneously deciding not to be the one who gasps.

MAYOR ANDERSON

(gripping the podium)

No — this is — this is a setup. I would never—

The footage keeps rolling. Somewhere a champagne glass breaks.

And then GAEL walks onto the stage.

Composed. Almost serene. He does not take the podium — he stands beside it, hands loose at his sides, and waits until the room's attention arrives on its own.

FADE OUT.

### Scene 15: Detective Office — Reaction

### Scene 17: Mayor's Office — Revelation

*EPISODE 3 — Blood and Weevils | ID: e3-mayors-office-revelation | File: e3_01_mayors_office_revelation.md*

---

<!-- summary: The Mayor tells Dustin the truth — the Weevil is his brother, Mark Anderson; the man on stage, Gael, runs Mark's operation. -->

INT. CITY HALL - MAYOR'S OFFICE - DAY

Quiet. Dark wood. Framed photographs of ribbon-cuttings and handshakes — a wall of achievements that reads, this morning, like a wall of evidence.

Dustin sits across the desk from Mayor Anderson. Between them, a bottle of good scotch, unopened. The Mayor has clearly considered it.

MAYOR ANDERSON

Dustin. I need to tell you the truth about the Weevil.

DUSTIN

I'm listening.

MAYOR ANDERSON

He's my brother. Mark. Mark Anderson.

Silence. Dustin absorbs this without moving. Behind his eyes, red string reconnecting.

MAYOR ANDERSON

He was always the smarter one. I was just the one people liked. You know what that does to a family — one boy gets the handshakes, the other gets the report cards, and everybody pretends it evens out.

He rises, moves to the window. The Green below, the city going about its morning.

MAYOR ANDERSON

Mark struggled. His mind — it went to dark rooms and stayed there. When it got bad enough, the family sent him to a facility in Maine. We told ourselves it was for his own good. We told ourselves that for eleven years.

DUSTIN

He got out.

MAYOR ANDERSON

He walked out. Took the family fortune with him — every account, cleaned with a patience that should have warned us. And then he built something. A following. Cash only, loyalty only. People with real grievances, and God knows this city manufactures those. He tells them he'll tear New Haven down and build it honest. He calls it a new city. They believe him.

DUSTIN

And the man on your stage last night? That's not Mark.

MAYOR ANDERSON

No. Mark doesn't step into rooms. Mark is the architect — you'll never see him holding the pen.

(turning from the window)

The man on the stage runs Mark's operation on the ground. Every job, every crew, every one of those little figurines. My people got one photograph and no name. He's a ghost.

DUSTIN

We have one of those too.

The Mayor doesn't hear it. His voice breaks — quietly, the way it does in men who are practiced at not letting it.

MAYOR ANDERSON

I failed him, Dustin. Whatever he's become, it started the day we put him in a car to Maine and I waved. I failed as a brother.

Dustin is quiet for a long moment. Then:

DUSTIN

Mayor. What's done is done. What's now — is now.

The Mayor looks at him. It is not absolution, but it is something a man can stand on.

FADE OUT.

### Scene 18: Dustin Hints at the Past

*EPISODE 3 — Blood and Weevils | ID: e3-dustin-hints-history | File: e3_02_dustin_hints_history.md*

---

<!-- summary: Dustin admits, almost to himself, that he knew someone connected to Mark a long time ago — and refuses to explain. -->

INT. CITY HALL - MAYOR'S OFFICE - CONTINUOUS

The Mayor pours two glasses of the scotch after all. Slides one across. Dustin doesn't touch it.

MAYOR ANDERSON

You understand what I'm asking you to carry. If it gets out that the Weevil is an Anderson—

DUSTIN

It won't get out from me.

MAYOR ANDERSON

Why do I believe you?

DUSTIN

Because I'm the only man in this city with less to gain from talking than you.

Connected how?

Dustin. Connected how?

It was a long time ago.

We all have our Maines, Detective.

He was right. And mine didn't have a facility or a family fortune. Mine had a name I hadn't said out loud in years — and I wasn't going to start in the Mayor's office.

### Scene 19: The Alliance Forms

INT. CITY HALL - MAYOR'S OFFICE - LATER

MAYOR ANDERSON

What I'm proposing is simple. A team. Off the books until it can't be — my resources, your skills. Everything Mark's network touches: the robberies, the blackmail, the intimidation on the waterfront, the people he's swallowed. We take it apart, piece by piece.

MAYOR ANDERSON

Three of whom aren't getting paid.

### Scene 20: Boatyard Trap

*EPISODE 3 — Blood and Weevils | ID: e3-boatyard-trap | File: e3_04_boatyard_trap.md*

---

EXT. BOATYARD - NIGHT

Salt air. Creaking docks. The team moves between the dry-docked hulls in a loose diamond — Dustin on point, the Ghost drifting at the flank, Lucy and the Mayor in the middle. Flashlights low. The Mayor wears a bulletproof vest over a dress shirt, which is somehow worse than either alone.

MAYOR ANDERSON

(whispering)

Is it always this quiet?

LUCY

(whispering)

No.

They round a hull into the open center of the yard. And stop.

A wooden DESK stands in the middle of the gravel, lit by a work lamp. On the desk, arranged in a neat row facing them: FOUR WEEVIL FIGURINES.

One for each of them.

DUSTIN

(low)

We've walked right into a trap.

GHOST

(undaunted)

Stay focused. We can handle this.

Movement. From behind the boats, out of the container shadows, from everywhere at once: HENCHMEN. A dozen of them, in matching WEEVIL COSTUMES — bulbous insect heads, segmented padding, each holding a baseball bat. The effect is absurd and, somehow, much worse for it. A ring closes around the team, unhurried, drilled.

Nobody speaks. The costumes make no sound. The lamp hums.

LUCY

(deadpan, terrified)

Oh good. There's a theme.

And then the ring opens at the far side, and a man steps through it.

No costume. A suit. Clean shoes on filthy gravel.

GAEL.

He stops beside the desk, in the lamplight, and looks at them each in turn — the Mayor, Lucy, the Ghost — and last, and longest, Dustin.

He looks at Dustin with something that isn't quite satisfaction and isn't quite regret.

### Scene 21: The Magnet / First-Name Moment

EXT. BOATYARD - CONTINUOUS

Gael reaches into his jacket and takes out a small DEVICE — matte, no lights, the size of a cigarette case. He handles it like a light switch.

He presses it.

A deep electromagnetic THUMP. Every gun in the team's possession WRENCHES free — Dustin's pistol, the Ghost's sidearm, the Mayor's borrowed revolver — and streaks through the air to SLAM against a rusted crane column behind Gael. LUCY

Our guns! He's—

MAYOR ANDERSON

We may have lost our weapons. But we still have each other.

LUCY

Mister Mayor. He watches your press conferences. He wants you to know that.

### Scene 22: The Fight

*EPISODE 3 — Blood and Weevils | ID: e3-fight | File: e3_06_fight.md*

---

<!-- summary: The team fights the weevil henchmen as a unit for the first time; Gael slips away, unhurried, gone. -->

EXT. BOATYARD - CONTINUOUS

The ring collapses inward and the fight begins.

It is not graceful. It is a real fight in a real boatyard — grabbed collars, swung bats, gravel. But something happens in it: the team works.

The Ghost moves like his name — never where the bat lands, guiding henchmen into each other with small economical redirections. He puts one into a stack of lobster traps.

Lucy fights like ten years of last call — a serving tray from the desk becomes a shield, then an argument-ender. A henchman goes down rubbing his bulbous mask.

The Mayor, to everyone's surprise including his own, tackles a henchman into a rack of oars. Buried in weevil, he throws punches with thirty years of suppressed press conferences behind them.

Dustin puts one henchman down, spins, drops under a bat—

—and comes up looking at Gael.

Gael has not moved. He stands outside the brawl, watching it the way a man watches rain — and watching Dustin most of all. Cataloguing. Almost fond.

Dustin starts toward him. A henchman intercepts; Dustin fights through him, shoves clear—

Gael is farther away. He hasn't run. He is simply, somehow, always farther away — through a gap between containers that was not visible until he used it. No scrambling. No panic. The exit of a man who planned it before he arrived.

At the gap, he pauses. Looks back at Dustin across the chaos. Then he is gone.

DUSTIN

(hoarse)

He slipped away again!

GHOST

(putting down the last henchman)

We'll get him next time.

The remaining henchmen break and retreat toward the dock exit — and as they go, they drag with them THREE BOUND CIVILIANS, hooded, stumbling. Hostages.

The team freezes.

### Scene 25: Final Handshake / Dustin Alone
