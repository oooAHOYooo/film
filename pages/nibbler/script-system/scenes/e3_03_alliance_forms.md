INT. CITY HALL - MAYOR'S OFFICE - LATER

The office, fuller now: Dustin, the Ghost by the window where he can see the door, Lucy in the good chair because she took it first.

MAYOR ANDERSON

What I'm proposing is simple. A team. Off the books until it can't be — my resources, your skills. Everything Mark's network touches: the robberies, the blackmail, the intimidation on the waterfront, the people he's swallowed. We take it apart, piece by piece.

He looks around the room.

MAYOR ANDERSON

I can't use the department. Half of them want my job and the other half think I'm guilty. What I have is the four people in this room.

LUCY

Three of whom aren't getting paid.

MAYOR ANDERSON

That can be discussed.

LUCY

Good. Because I quit shanty night for this.

GHOST

What's our exposure? When this goes wrong — and it will go wrong — who takes the fall?

MAYOR ANDERSON

I do. That's the arrangement, and I'll put it in writing. I'm done letting other people carry what's mine.

The Ghost studies him a moment. Then gives his single nod.

The Mayor turns to Dustin.

MAYOR ANDERSON

Detective? It's your call. It was always going to be your call.

DUSTIN

I'm in.

He said it before the Mayor finished the sentence. Everyone notices.

DUSTIN

(V.O.)

Fifteen years I'd worked this city alone — one man, one magnifying glass, one bar that poured before I asked. Now I had a crew. A ghost, a bartender, and the most hated man in New Haven.

(beat)

In this town, that's practically an army.

The Ghost unrolls a chart on the desk: the boatyard, marked in grease pencil.

GHOST

Signal traffic spiked again last night. Same source. The boatyard.

DUSTIN

Then that's where we start.

FADE OUT.
