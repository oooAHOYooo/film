INT. DALLS ATTIC - MORNING (SCENE 17A)

Dallas looks at the creature by himself for a long time. 

Dallas stares for a long time at the golden creature. The jagged red lines on his portable viewfinder match the erratic, panicked breathing of the tiny animal. 

The creature is not doing so good - but seems to be dying - and the distant hum perks up again. 

Dallas walks over to the workbench. Makayla's soother sits where he left it. He picks it up, turns it
over in his hands — studies the wiring, the dial, the way the VU meter is mounted. Then he sets it
back down and reaches for Sierra's cassette recorder beside it.

He presses RECORD. Points the microphone toward the creature.

The creature opens its mouth and emits a low, melodic tone — the same frequency Makayla's rig coaxed
out of it. As it sings, the golden glow in its neck grows stronger, pushing back the shadows in the
room.

Dallas watches the VU needle climb. He watches the glow rise and fall with the tone. He is not
discovering this — he is documenting it. Writing it down in the only language that matters.

When the tape runs out he stops recording. Sits for a long time looking at the cassette in his hand.
Sierra's recorder. Her tape. The creature's voice on it now.

Dallas at his workstation. Headphones on. The cassette playing back through the spectrogram — the
creature's call rendered as a waveform on his laptop screen. He studies the peaks. Isolates the
fundamental frequency. Begins to process it — layering, amplifying, stripping the noise until what
remains is clean and pure and loud enough to fill a marsh.

Dallas transfers the frequency - and begins to add it to the receiver. 

He walks out the door. He carefully puts the creature inside of his backpack.

EXT. BRANFORD HARBOR - DAY (SCENE 17C)

Dominic walks the docks at the Branford town marina, a clipboard under his arm. The harbor is buzzing with the banal energy of pre-storm preparation—owners lashing extra lines, tying down tarps, hauling small craft up the ramp.

(action)
Dominic moves down the row of slips, checking each boat, signing off on slip paperwork as he goes. Routine. Calm.

But beneath it all—beneath the slap of halyards against masts, the gulls, the chatter of the boatyard—Dominic hears the HUM. 

It is distant. Ambient. It rides in low over the water, almost like the drone of a far-off outboard, but too rhythmic. He stops at the end of the dock and stares out across the chop, trying to isolate the sound. He shakes his head, actively gaslighting himself. Just the wind in the rigging.

(action)

DOMINIC
Yeah.
