(action)
The mother is failing. The Red-Eyes pile onto her, and her golden light dims under the weight of them.

(action)
Dallas drops the oscillator dials and tears open the field bag.

The blaster. Makayla's soother, rebuilt. And loaded in its deck — Sierra's cassette. Her recorder.
Her tape. The creature's song, caught by her hands.

He hauls it up, braces it against his chest, thumb on the taped toggle — a weapon, aimed at the
swarm, one more scream against the storm—

—and he stops.

(action)
Through the wall of thrashing red, for half a second, he can see her. The mother. Curled around the
baby, her light guttering, taking it all on her own back.

Not fighting. Shielding.

Dallas looks down at the rig in his hands. At what he built it to be.

He lowers it.

DOMINIC
(screaming over the wind)
DALLAS! USE IT!

(action)
Dallas kneels in the mud. He sets the blaster down flat in the flattened grass, facing the mother.

He turns the gain down. All the way down. Barely there — the way Makayla played it the first time,
in a basement, to a frightened baby.

He presses PLAY.

(action)
Under the roar of the storm and the rustle of the swarm, so quiet it should be nothing:

Sierra's recording. The creature's song, caught clean. An offering, not a weapon.

For a moment, nothing.

Then the mother's head turns.

Through the pile of bramble and burning red eyes, through her own failing light — she hears it. A
song she has heard before. Years before. Caught by a woman who knelt in this same grass with a
recorder in her hands and asked for nothing.

The mother's eyes find Dallas.

And her light SURGES.

(action)
Far out in the dark, one of the spherical recorders tears up off its stake.

Then another. Then a dozen. Then the whole field.

She is calling them. Every machine they ever planted to hunt her — she pulls them out of the reeds
and gathers them to her, and they come.
