INT. DOMINIC'S KITCHEN / LIVING ROOM - NIGHT (s18.1)

Families arrive in waves. The small kitchen and smaller living room fill. The crowd tries Dominic's
toasted Brussels sprouts, passed around on paper plates. Brownies and popcorn crowd the kitchen
island. Conversation hums. Casual. Normal.

JOSH, 36, tall and blonde, bounces newborn JP on his shoulder and talks with PAT CLENDENEN, 60s.

JOSH
Think it'll flood?

PAT
Definitely. The boys in the office say the pressure's dropping fast. It'll hit us some point tonight.

(action)
Dominic moves through the crowd — greeting, refilling, hosting. Every so often his eyes flick to the
basement door.

The doorbell.

INT. DOMINIC'S HALLWAY - CONTINUOUS (s18.2)

Dominic opens the door. Dallas — last to arrive, rain on his shoulders, the heavy field bag on his
back. Nobody clocks the bag except Dominic. Dallas pulls him to the corner.

DALLAS
(low, urgent)
It's agitated. The hum outside — it's answering it. I need to be down there.

DOMINIC
(strained smile)
Then be down there quietly. I have forty people standing on top of you.

(action)
Dallas slips through the basement door and eases it shut behind him. Dominic turns back into the
party wearing a host's smile.

INT. DOMINIC'S BASEMENT - CONTINUOUS (s18.3)

The creature is in the shoebox under the heat lamp, right where Dallas left it. But its breathing is
fast. Erratic. Its glow flickers like a failing bulb.

Dallas unpacks the rig fast — receiver, oscillator, headphones — a secret emergency room assembled
under a church social. On the portable oscilloscope, the creature's pulse spikes in jagged lines.

DALLAS
(soft, working)
I'm here. You're safe.

(action)
He keys the soother — the creature's own call, played back low and warm. The creature settles. The
jagged line rounds off. It's working.

(action)
Then, underneath it, the scope picks up a second trace. Fainter. Outside. Matching the broadcast
note for note.

Dallas stares at it. Something out there is singing back.

INT. DOMINIC'S LIVING ROOM - CONTINUOUS (s18.4)

The group settles into a circle. TYLER, 32, guitar on his knee, hands out music sheets.

TYLER
Alright, crew. Glad we're all gathered — on this breezy summer night.

(action)
The wind gusts against the windows on cue. The group laughs.

TYLER
Should we wait for the new guy? Dallas, is it?

DOMINIC
(quick)
He's downstairs, actually. We found a stray cat outside — it's really sick. He's nursing it.

(action)
MARY, 50s, warm, lowers her music sheet.

MARY
I'm a retired vet. I could take a look.

DOMINIC
No — no, that's okay. I think it's finally sleeping. Maybe after?

(action)
Mary nods, satisfied. Dominic exhales.

Across the room, MAKAYLA stands apart from the circle, near the window. She isn't holding a music
sheet. One hand is in her jacket pocket, closed around her tracker. Her eyes are on the basement door.

TYLER
Would anyone like to say any words before we start?

(action)
A beat of bowed heads. Then Tyler strums, and the group begins: "Great Is Thy Faithfulness..."

INT. DOMINIC'S BASEMENT - CONTINUOUS (s18.5)

The hymn bleeds down through the floorboards, muffled and warm.

The creature's head turns toward the ceiling. Its glow pulses in time with the voices — then against
them, off-beat, straining. On the scope, the outside trace swells.

(action)
Dallas eases the gain down. The creature won't settle. The broadcast, the hymn, the thing outside —
three songs fighting over one small body.

He clamps the headphones tighter, chasing the outside trace across frequencies. It isn't ambient.
It's closer than it was ten minutes ago.

And the creature is no longer looking at the ceiling. It is looking at the hopper window.

INT. DOMINIC'S LIVING ROOM - CONTINUOUS (s18.6)

The group sings, voices rising into the second verse.

Underneath the hymn, barely audible at first, a LOW HUM begins to resonate through the floor.

SARAH, sitting nearest the floor with her tea, feels it first. The surface of the tea ripples —
concentric rings, expanding in perfect time. Each time the hymn hits a high note, the hum swells to
meet it.

SARAH
(quiet, to Dominic)
Is that—

DOMINIC
(quickly)
The storm. Wind in the house.

(action)
The overhead lights dim and recover. The window glass rattles in its frame. A few voices falter.

PAT
Must be the wind. Pressure's dropping fast — the boys just texted me. It's going to be a rough one.

(action)
By the window, Makayla slides the tracker from her pocket and checks it below the counter line.

On the screen: the creature's dot is no longer inside the house.

It is out. Moving. A clean straight line away from the neighborhood — toward the center of the
marsh. And around it, red blips winking on. One. Four. A dozen. Converging on the same point from
every side.

(action)
Makayla crosses to Dominic. She opens a worn waterproof notebook — held low, where only he can see.
A pencil sketch of bent reeds, dated 1971. One underlined line in careful handwriting: WRONG SONG
BRINGS THE WRONG GUESTS.

MAKAYLA
(low)
It's out. It's going to the center. And they know.

(action)
Dominic looks down to wave her off — and stops.

He knows that handwriting. He has known it his whole life. Birthday cards. Grocery lists. The inside
covers of hymnals.

His mother's.

He looks at the notebook. At the date. At his sister — and sees her, for one second, all the way
through: the basement, the dinners carried down, the years of it. Everything he filed away without
reading.

DOMINIC
(barely getting it out)
Not. Right. Now.

(action)
Makayla looks at him for a moment. She doesn't argue twice.

She closes the notebook and puts it away. She sets down nothing — she never held a music sheet.
She crosses the living room, quiet and even, opens the front door, and walks out into the storm
mid-verse.

Wind and rain gust into the room. The door swings shut behind her.

The circle keeps singing, thinner now. A few heads turn. An upset girl, stepping out. It happens.

Dominic stands with the hymn in his mouth and his mother's handwriting behind his eyes, and he
cannot move. He is the host. There is a secret under his floor and forty people on top of it.

He mouths the words, not making a sound, sweat at his temple.

INT. DOMINIC'S BASEMENT - CONTINUOUS (s18.7)

A gust of wet wind lifts the papers on the bench.

Dallas rips off the headphones and turns. The hopper window at the top of the wall is OPEN — swung
wide, rain blowing in. Makayla's wedge lies snapped on the floor beneath it.

The shoebox is empty.

DALLAS
No. No no no—

(action)
He lunges to the laptop and pulls up the tracker feed from the tag in the creature's fur.

One signal, moving fast and straight — across the yards, into the grass, toward the center of the
marsh. It is not fleeing. It is going somewhere.

Around it, red blips. Dozens. Converging on the same point from every side.

And behind it, a third dot. Already outside. Already moving. The same heading.

Makayla.

(action)
Dallas looks at the screen one more second — the small gold signal, the closing red ring, the girl
walking into the middle of it.

He shoulders the field bag. The blaster shifts inside it against his back.

He takes the stairs two at a time.

INT. DOMINIC'S LIVING ROOM - CONTINUOUS (s18.8)

The hymn falters and dies mid-verse as the basement door BANGS open.

Dallas stands in it, soaked from the window spray, the empty shoebox in one hand, the field bag on
his back. He is not glassy-eyed. He is terrified and completely awake.

DALLAS
(to Dominic, across the whole room)
It's out. It's headed for the marsh — and Makayla's already after it.

(action)
Silence. Forty faces. Sarah stands, pulling Josh's arm. Mary's hand goes to her mouth. The hum under
the floor is undeniable now — rhythmic, structured, rising.

Dominic looks at the door his sister walked through. Then at the room.

The host's smile comes off like a coat.

DOMINIC
(to the room, fast and final)
Storm's getting bad. Everybody home, right now. Drive safe — text me when you're in.

(action)
It's the truth they can accept, and they take it. Coats, casserole dishes, JP crying, headlights.
The group pours out into the rain.

Mary pauses at the door, looking back at Dominic.

MARY
Honey — your sister—

DOMINIC
I'm getting her.

EXT. DOMINIC'S DRIVEWAY / STREET - CONTINUOUS (s18.9)

Taillights pull away through the rain, one after another, down the street toward town.

Dominic runs the other way.

Down the middle of the street, against the current of his own departing guests, coat forgotten,
shouting into the wind—

DOMINIC
MAKAYLA!

(action)
Headlights sweep across him as the cars pass — the host, mask fully off, in front of every person
he spent the whole night lying to. He doesn't care. He runs.

Past the last parked car, the street ends and the world opens: the black field, the wall of reeds
thrashing at the edge of it.

And there — small, already at the grass line — Makayla. Gear bag on her shoulder. Tracker glowing
in her hand.

She doesn't turn around.

She steps into the tall grass and the grass closes behind her.

DOMINIC
(gutted, to no one)
Makayla—

(action)
Footsteps behind him. Dallas catches up, breathing hard, the field bag on his back, rain streaming
off both of them.

They look at each other for one beat. There is no version of this where either of them goes home.

DALLAS
She's headed for the blind.

(action)
They go in after her.
